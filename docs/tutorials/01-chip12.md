# Tutorial 1: Chip 12

`examples/tutorials/01_chip12_coupled_chip.ipynb` rebuilds a legacy nine-qubit test chip, "chip 12", in the Quantcore
PDK, step by step, and runs the whole loop on it. The chip is an internal Quantum Metal design; its positions and
options are in `examples/designs/legacy/_legacy_b.py` and its spec is [Chip 12](../designs/legacy/d12_coupled_chip.md).
The design notebook builds the same chip in one cell; this tutorial builds it in small steps. It is the first
tutorial where the layout comes from a source and the PDK rules change it.

```{figure} ../images/tutorials/t01_chip.png
:width: 55%

Section 6: all nine qubits, twelve launchpads, readouts and control lines, with device ID `D12`.
```

## What the chip is

A 7.5 × 7.5 mm coupled-qubit test chip:

- one 20/12 µm feedline across the die centre with nine λ/4 readouts, 4.3 to 6.3 mm long;
- nine `TransmonCross` Xmons (`metal:` wrapper);
- **Q1-Q2**, a direct pair: their arm tips face each other across a 20 µm ground strip;
- **Q5-Q8-Q6**, a tunable-coupler triple: Q8 is a flux-tunable Xmon between Q5 and Q6, with its own readout;
- five flux lines and five drive lines, each to its own launchpad (ten line pads and two feed pads).

It is a PDK rebuild, not a replica. The PDK wins where it has a rule:

| | From the source | What the PDK changes |
|---|---|---|
| Pads | Legacy launchpad positions | Outer etch edge 100 µm from the die edge |
| Junctions | External junction cells, no values | Trilayer `Quantcore Junction` / `Quantcore Squid` at the PDK nominal R_n 45 kΩ (L_J 6.73 nH) |
| Cheese | None | The PDK band around the 3/0 keep-outs |
| Readouts | Lengths and couplers | Couplers pinned with `near=` so they stay put when pads move |
| Values | No qubit targets | Out-of-margin values are flagged (DRC design warnings), never retuned |

### 3 to 6: pads, feedline, qubits, routes

Twelve pads are added in one `with d.batch():` block, so the design is rebuilt once instead of twelve times. Every pad
is asked at its legacy position and slid to the 100 µm rule. The feed pads are 400 × 400 µm (20/12 µm trace); the ten
line pads are 400 × 300 µm (10/6 µm trace).

The Xmons have a 220 × 50 µm cross in a 20 µm gap and a 150 µm readout claw (6 µm gap). Q1-Q2, Q5-Q8 and Q8-Q6 couple
through their facing arm tips; no route joins them, so `d.couple(...)` declares each with a coupled-strip estimate of
1.67 fF. The `Q1_Q2` FEM group gives the real value.

Readout lengths came out equal to the legacy ones. The flux and drive lines run through the legacy corner points as
waypoints; the pads moved to the edge, so the first straight of each line is simply longer.

### 7 to 9: DRC, netlist and the LOM

The legacy readout lengths were drawn for a bare λ/4 (no claw), so each legacy length's bare frequency is the readout
target. Each qubit with its readout, the feedline acting as a load:

| Qubit | f_q (GHz) | α (MHz) | Readout f_r, LOM (target) GHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q0 | 5.0591 | -196.9 | 4.9357 (5.7361) |
| Q1 | 5.0411 | -146 | 4.4242 (5.0317) |
| Q2 | 5.0401 | -145.2 | 4.5015 (5.1317) |
| Q3 | 5.0325 | -146 | 5.7585 (6.7936) |
| Q4 | 5.0331 | -146.4 | 5.937 (7.041) |
| Q5 | 5.0248 | -135.7 | 5.226 (6.0594) |
| Q6 | 5.0277 | -139.6 | 5.283 (6.1396) |
| Q7 | 5.046 | -124.6 | 4.8085 (5.5375) |
| Q8 | 5.0352 | -146.8 | 6.1513 (7.3276) |

Every Xmon lands near 5.0 GHz (inside the PDK band) with α about -145 MHz. The claw adds about 125 fF at each
readout's open end, so the loaded readouts sit 0.6 to 1.2 GHz below their bare λ/4. Q0 and R0 come out nearly
degenerate and hybridise (χ = 64.7 MHz), and Q7 and R7 are close (χ = -22.7 MHz). The notebook does not retune them:
that is a design decision.

The direct pair Q1-Q2, with the same junction on both, shows two hybridised modes at 5.1220 and 5.1828 GHz and
J = 29.5 MHz. That J comes from the rough 1.7 fF arm estimate. With Q2 detuned (L_J 7.4 nH) the static ZZ is
-1.12e+04 kHz, in the MHz range: the reason Q8 sits between Q5 and Q6 as a tunable coupler. The triple at zero flux
(all three resonant) gives three hybridised modes at 5.0932, 5.1529 and 5.1836 GHz.

### 10 and 11: the FEM stages

The sim groups cut the chip into jobs a workstation can solve:

| Group | Analysis | Box | Gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Q1_R1` and one `Qn_Rn` per qubit | `q3d_cmatrix` | The qubit and 300 µm; the readout is cut at the claw | C_island, C_g, C_claw |
| `Q1_q` | `eigenmode_epr` | Q1 and 150 µm; the readout and flux lines leave the box as 50 Ω lines | The qubit's own mode: f_q, α, p_J |
| `Q1_R1e` | `eigenmode_epr` | Q1, its whole readout and 100 µm, with the feedline cut at both ends | f_q, f_r, χ, κ |
| `Q1_Q2` | `q3d_cmatrix` | Both qubits and 300 µm | The arm-to-arm capacitance, hence J |
| `Q5_Q8_Q6` | `q3d_cmatrix` | The triple and 300 µm | The tunable-coupler capacitances |

The eigenmode job of the whole `Q1_R1` box (a 1.3 × 2.9 mm box) did not finish within 20 minutes on the workstation,
which is why the notebook splits it into `Q1_q` and `Q1_R1e`.

The kept results (fast settings):

| Job | Passes | Last Δ / target | Converged | Wall time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Q1_R1` Q3D | 8 | 1.24 / 2.0 % | no | 60.6 s |
| `Q1_Q2` Q3D | 8 | 1.33 / 2.0 % | no | 41.1 s |
| `Q1_q` eigenmode | 3 | 0.591 / 1.0 % | yes | 123.6 s |
| `Q1_R1e` eigenmode | 3 | 1.67 / 1.0 % | no | 276.8 s |

The `Q1_R1` C-matrix gives C_g 10.883 fF against the 5.2 fF estimate and C_claw 111.388 fF. The eigenmode of Q1 alone
gives a dressed f_q of 4.7564 GHz and α of -126.9 MHz (p_J 0.9876). `Q1_R1e` gives f_q 4.7212 GHz and a cross-Kerr
χ of -26.451 MHz.

Stage 2 fills the branches the Q3D jobs simulated (9 branches) into the whole netlist; 40 branches keep their
estimates. The LOM then runs pair by pair, because QuCat on all nine qubits at once is far too slow. For Q1 and Q2
the LOM on the subset Q3D moves f_q by +1.6 % and +3.1 %, and the readout χ from -1.644 to -9.158 MHz (Q1) and from
-2.369 to -10.86 MHz (Q2). Against pyEPR on `Q1_q` the filled LOM f_q is 7.2 % high (5.123 against 4.756 GHz). For
the pair, the FEM arm-to-arm capacitance is 1.153 fF (against 1.67 fF) and J drops from 29.53 to 21.91 MHz.

Feedback moves each readout's length: the λ/4 scales with f / target. For example R0 goes from 5493 to 4727 µm and R8
from 4300 to 3610 µm. The result is a revision `examples/tutorials/designs/t01_chip12_fb.json` (DRC clean), exported
to a fab file. The consensus on the whole netlist reports 0 of 9 targets within tolerance: the revision is the first step, and
the loop closes by running stage 2 on it.

### 13: export

`export_fab` keeps the build's cheese (14723 holes) and rounds corners. With `cheese=False, rounding=False` it writes
0 holes and 42 `M1` polygons. The CLI equivalents are in the notebook:
`qcc build examples/tutorials/designs/t01_chip12.json --show`, `qcc design drc ...`, `qcc circuit export ...`,
`qcc circuit simulate ...`.

## Pitfalls

- Hangers by arclength (`s=`) slide when a pad moves; use `near=` as the notebook does.
- `squid_a` and `squid_b` are junction attachment points, not route ends. They show as `dangling_port` warnings. That
  is expected.
- A SQUID gets the same total L_J as a single junction, so each of its two junctions has twice the R_n. The PDK window
  judges the device's parallel R_n.
- At a 200 µm claw the claw would reach the cross's side arms, and the DRC flags it as `pdk_claw`. The notebook stops at 180 µm.
- The FEM results of sections 10 and 11 belong to the original geometry. After section 12 changes the design hash they are stale: re-run them to refresh.
- The Q3D jobs are fast-setting results, not converged (Δ 1.2 to 1.3 % against 2 %).
- `FEM_WHOLE = True` also solves the whole chip as one Q3D (`chip_q3d_cmatrix` is kept). Whole-chip eigenmode or
  driven runs take hours and over 30 GB.
